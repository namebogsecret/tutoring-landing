Key facts

  • AP Physics C is a college-level, calculus-based course
  • Format (per College Board, for the May 2027 exam): Section I — 42 multiple-choice questions, 85 minutes, 50%; Section II — 4 free-response questions, 95 minutes, 50%
  • Calculators are allowed
  • Mechanics: 7 units; Electricity and Magnetism: 6 units
  • Lesson language: Russian, English or mixed

Units

Mechanics: kinematics; force and translational dynamics; work, energy and power; linear momentum; torque and rotational dynamics; energy and momentum of rotating systems; oscillations. Electricity and Magnetism: electric charges, fields and Gauss's law; electric potential; conductors and capacitors; electric circuits; magnetic fields; electromagnetic induction.

What we do in lessons

We work through the units, practise the four free-response question types (mathematical derivations, translation between representations, experimental design, qualitative-quantitative problems) and sit timed full papers. 10-minute diagnostic

Five questions on the topic of this page, one correct answer out of four each. Answers can be changed; the button shows the result once all five are answered; it is a guide to core topics, not a prediction of an exam grade.

  1. A truck collides with a small car. How do the magnitudes of the forces they exert on each other during the collision compare?

  2. A 2 kg object speeds up from 3 m/s to 5 m/s. How much work does the net force do?

  3. A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s collides with a stationary 1 kg cart and the carts stick together. What is their common speed after the collision?

  4. A ball on a string moves in a horizontal circle at constant speed. What is the direction of the net force on the ball?

  5. A mass on a spring undergoes simple harmonic motion. The mass is increased by a factor of 4 with the same spring. What happens to the period?
