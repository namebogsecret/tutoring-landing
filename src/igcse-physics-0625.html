What the syllabus covers

The Cambridge IGCSE Physics syllabus (0625) for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028 is built around six topics:

  1. Motion, forces and energy
  2. Thermal physics
  3. Waves
  4. Electricity and magnetism
  5. Nuclear physics
  6. Space physics

The syllabus is written for about 130 guided learning hours; that figure is Cambridge's guide to the size of the course, not a target for tutoring hours. The Extended tier is the Core content plus the Supplement.

Core or Extended

A candidate enters one tier, and the tier decides which papers are sat and which grades are available.

  • Core — Paper 1 (multiple choice, 45 minutes, 40 marks, 30% of the grade) plus Paper 3 (theory, 1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50%) plus one practical paper, Paper 5 or Paper 6 (20%). Grades available: C to G. This is the usual route for candidates expecting a D or below.
  • Extended (Core plus Supplement) — Paper 2 (multiple choice, 45 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) plus Paper 4 (theory, 1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50%) plus one practical paper, Paper 5 or Paper 6 (20%). Grades available: A* to G. This is the usual route for candidates expecting a C or higher.

Multiple-choice questions have four answer options. Choosing the tier is a decision to make with the school early, because the two tiers are prepared differently: Extended needs the Supplement content and the longer calculation and explanation answers of Paper 4.

🧪

The practical paper is 20% of the grade. Paper 5 (Practical Test, 1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks) and Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical, 1 hour, 40 marks) each carry the same 20% weight, and a candidate takes one of them. Both assess the experimental skills set out in section 4 of the syllabus.

Paper 5 vs Paper 6

Paper 5 is a practical test: the candidate carries out experiments, takes readings and records results. Paper 6 is the written alternative: the same skills are assessed on paper, with a described experiment, data tables and graphs to analyse. Either way the skills are the same: planning, taking and recording measurements, drawing graphs, analysing data and evaluating a method. Check with the school early which of the two will be sat, so the preparation can focus on the right format.

How marks are earned: AO1, AO2, AO3

Cambridge describes three assessment objectives, and they explain why the same physics can score differently depending on how it is used.

  • AO1 — knowledge with understanding: 50%.
  • AO2 — handling information and problem solving: 30%.
  • AO3 — experimental skills and investigations: 20%.

Half of the marks reward knowing and understanding the content, but the other half reward using it: working with data, solving problems and handling experimental work. Lessons are therefore built around past-paper-style questions, not only around explanations.

How IGCSE Physics differs from the Russian school course

Students who come from a Russian school often have a strong mathematical base, and that is a real advantage. The differences are mostly in format and expectations rather than in how hard the physics is: IGCSE uses multiple-choice papers alongside theory papers, and it treats experimental skills as a separate 20% of the grade. The topic order and the depth of individual topics also differ from the Russian programme. A short diagnostic on the first lessons shows which topics are already covered and which need work. How the work is organised

  • Diagnostic. A set of questions across the six topics shows what is secure and what is not, and helps to settle Core or Extended.
  • Plan by topic. The six topics are scheduled against the exam date, with extra time on the Supplement content for Extended candidates.
  • Lessons and practice. One-to-one online lessons, worked examples, then past-paper-style questions marked against the mark scheme logic: what the question asks for, which formula, which unit, which wording earns the mark.
  • Practical skills. Regular work on the format of Paper 5 or Paper 6 (tables, graphs, uncertainty in readings, evaluating a method), so the 20% practical component is not left to the last weeks.
  • Timed papers. Full papers under exam timing (45 minutes for the multiple-choice paper, 1 hour 15 minutes for theory) and a review of every lost mark.

No results are promised: they depend on the starting point, the time available and regular work between lessons.
