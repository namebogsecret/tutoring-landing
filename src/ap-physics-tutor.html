Physics 1, 2 and C — which is which

The four AP Physics exams ask for different things, and the right preparation looks different for each:

CourseMathsCore focus
Physics 1AlgebraKinematics, dynamics, energy, momentum, rotation, SHM
Physics 2AlgebraFluids, thermodynamics, electricity, circuits, optics, modern
Physics C: MechanicsCalculusMotion, forces, energy, momentum, rotation — with derivatives & integrals
Physics C: E&MCalculusFields, potential, capacitance, circuits, magnetism, induction
∫

Physics C is where calculus and physics collide. Most lost marks live exactly there — taking derivatives of position for motion, integrating charge for fields and potential. I teach the calculus and the physics together, so the maths becomes a tool, not a second exam.

The free-response section — written to the rubric

FRQs are scored point by point against a published rubric, and the rubric rewards a structure: name the principle, write the governing relation, then substitute with units. We drill released College Board FRQs, mark them exactly as a reader would, and fix the quiet habits that cost points — missing units, dropped signs, "describe" answers to "justify" prompts, and skipping the algebra before plugging numbers.

The multiple-choice section — speed without panic

The MCQ section rewards clean concepts under time pressure. We build that with timed sets, distractor analysis (the wrong answers are designed around the common misconception), and fluency with the equation sheet so you spend thinking time on physics, not on hunting for a formula.
