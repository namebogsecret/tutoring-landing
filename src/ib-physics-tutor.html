IB Physics is not hard because the physics is exotic. It is hard because three things have to land at once: the concepts, the command terms that tell you exactly what the examiner wants, and the Internal Assessment that quietly carries 20% of your grade. Most students are stronger than their predicted grade — they are losing marks they already understand.

IB Physics HL and SL — what actually differs

SL and HL share the same core; HL adds the Additional Higher Level (AHL) topics and a deeper, more quantitative Paper 3. The grade boundaries are different, the time pressure on HL Paper 1 is real, and the AHL material (fields, induction, quantum and nuclear depth) is where HL grades are won or lost. We map every lesson to your exact subject guide and to the assessment objectives — AO1 (recall), AO2 (apply), AO3 (analyse and evaluate) — because the exam scores those, not vibes.

🎯

Command terms are the hidden syllabus. "State", "describe", "explain", "deduce" and "evaluate" each demand a different answer length and structure. Getting these right is often a one-band improvement on Paper 2 with no new physics learned.

The Internal Assessment — the most reliable marks in the course

The IA is the single best place to add points, because you control it. I help you pick a genuinely researchable question, design a method with a real independent variable, handle uncertainties and error propagation correctly (this is where IB IAs leak marks), and structure the analysis and evaluation against the rubric — personal engagement, exploration, analysis, evaluation, communication. I coach it; you write it. That line matters for academic honesty and it matters for your learning.
