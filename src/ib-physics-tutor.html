The IB Physics course was rewritten for first assessment in 2025, so older notes, textbooks and past papers do not always match what is examined now. Most lost marks come from three places: not knowing which material is SL and which is HL-only, treating the data-based questions of Paper 1B as an afterthought, and starting the internal assessment too late. All three are easier once the structure of the course is clear.

The five themes

According to the Physics guide for first assessment 2025, the syllabus is organised into five themes, which the guide says are not a teaching order:

  1. Space, time and motion — includes kinematics and rigid body mechanics.
  2. The particulate nature of matter
  3. Wave behaviour
  4. Fields — includes gravitational fields and induction.
  5. Nuclear and quantum physics — includes radioactive decay and fission.

The guide connects the themes through three concepts: energy, particles and forces. The recommended teaching time is 150 hours for SL and 240 hours for HL. Of these, the syllabus content takes 110 hours at SL and 180 hours at HL, and the rest is the experimental programme: practical work, the collaborative sciences project and the scientific investigation. That figure is the IB's recommendation for schools, not a target for tutoring hours.

Where the HL time goes

The guide gives recommended hours for each theme. They show where HL adds the most material:

  • Space, time and motion — 27 hours at SL, 42 at HL.
  • The particulate nature of matter — 24 at SL, 32 at HL.
  • Wave behaviour — 17 at SL, 29 at HL.
  • Fields — 19 at SL, 38 at HL.
  • Nuclear and quantum physics — 23 at SL, 39 at HL.

Fields is the theme in which the recommended time doubles from SL to HL, and wave behaviour and the theme of space, time and motion also grow noticeably. A student moving from SL to HL, or choosing between them, can use this as a map of how much new material to expect.

Paper 1 and Paper 2

External assessment is 80% of the grade, and it consists of two papers.

  • SL — external assessment of 3 hours. Paper 1 (1 hour 30 minutes, 45 marks, 36%): Paper 1A has 25 multiple-choice questions and Paper 1B has data-based questions worth 20 marks. Paper 2 (1 hour 30 minutes, 50 marks, 44%): short-answer and extended-response questions on SL material only.
  • HL — external assessment of 4 hours 30 minutes. Paper 1 (2 hours, 60 marks, 36%): Paper 1A has 40 multiple-choice questions and Paper 1B has data-based questions worth 20 marks. Paper 2 (2 hours 30 minutes, 90 marks, 44%): short-answer and extended-response questions on SL and additional HL material.

Paper 1A and Paper 1B are completed together without interruption. No marks are deducted for incorrect answers in Paper 1A, so every multiple-choice question should be answered. Calculators are permitted, and each student needs a clean copy of the Physics data booklet. Learning to find quantities in that booklet quickly is a small habit that saves real minutes.

📊

Paper 1B is where data skills are tested. It is worth 20 marks at both levels and is taken immediately after the multiple-choice part, in the same sitting. Reading a graph, linearising a relationship and handling uncertainty are therefore exam skills, not only laboratory skills.

The internal assessment: the scientific investigation

The internal assessment is 20% of the grade, it is the same at SL and at HL, and it consists of one task, the scientific investigation. It is 10 hours of the course and carries 24 marks. The teacher marks it and the IB moderates it externally. It is assessed against four criteria: research design, data analysis, conclusion and evaluation.

The work must be the student's own, and the guide says that a teacher reads one draft and advises on how it could be improved, but does not edit it. Outside help works the same way. A tutor can discuss what makes a question researchable, how to plan the method, how to propagate uncertainties and how to judge an evaluation, but the investigation, the data and the text are the student's own. Lessons are used to build those skills so that the student can do the work independently.

How marks are earned: AO1 to AO3

The assessment objectives are demonstrating knowledge (AO1), understanding and applying knowledge (AO2), and analysing, evaluating and synthesising (AO3). The guide gives the approximate weighting for both papers as 50% for AO1 and AO2 together and 50% for AO3. In practice half of each paper rewards using physics on unfamiliar data and situations rather than recalling it. The internal assessment also covers a fourth objective, AO4, the skills needed to carry out insightful and ethical investigations. The guide also provides a table of command terms, such as describe, explain and justify, and each is tied to an assessment objective. Answering exactly what the command term asks for is one of the quickest ways to stop dropping marks.

How the work is organised

  • Diagnostic. A set of questions across the five themes shows what is secure, and for SL or HL candidates it shows which of the extra HL material is still missing.
  • Plan by theme. Themes are scheduled against the exam date, with more time on the ones that are heaviest at the student's level.
  • Lessons and practice. One-to-one online lessons with a shared whiteboard, worked examples, then exam-style questions marked against the logic of a mark scheme: what the command term asks for, which relation, which unit, which wording earns the mark.
  • Data skills. Regular work on graphs, uncertainties and data-based questions in the format of Paper 1B.
  • Investigation support. Discussion of the plan and the evaluation at each stage, within the limits described above.
  • Timed papers. Full Paper 1 and Paper 2 under exam timing, followed by a review of every lost mark.

No results are promised: they depend on the starting point, the time available and regular work between lessons.
