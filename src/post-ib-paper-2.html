IB Physics Paper 2: how to structure long-answer questions

Vladimir Podlevskikh · 9 June 2026

On Paper 2, strong students lose marks not because they don't know the physics, but because they answer a different question than the one asked. The fix is mechanical: read the command term, match the answer's shape to it, and show working the examiner can award point by point.

Command terms are the real question

Every IB Physics question is built around a command term that tells you exactly how much to write and in what form. Answer the term, not the topic:

Command termWhat the examiner wants
State / DefineOne line. No explanation, no working.
Determine / CalculateA number with units — and the working that earns method marks.
DescribeWhat happens, step by step. No "because".
ExplainWhat happens and why — the causal link is the mark.
Outline / SuggestA brief reasoned point; partial credit for a plausible mechanism.
🎯

The most common Paper 2 error: answering "explain" with a "describe". If the question says explain and your answer has no "because / therefore / due to", you've capped your own marks no matter how correct the physics is.

Structure for "determine / calculate"

Numerical parts are scored for method, not just the final number. Lay them out so each mark is visible and a small slip can't cost everything:

  • Name the principle — "Using conservation of momentum…".
  • Write the relation — the equation in symbols, before numbers.
  • Substitute with units — then evaluate.
  • State the answer — correct sig-figs, correct unit, sensible magnitude.

This earns the error-carried-forward rule: if you mis-key one number but the method is shown, you still bank the method marks and the answer marks downstream. Students who write only a final number get nothing when it's wrong.

Structure for "explain"

An "explain" answer is a short causal chain. Build it as: observation → physical cause → consequence. For example, "the gas cools (observation) because it does work on the surroundings as it expands (cause), so its internal energy and therefore temperature fall (consequence)." Three linked clauses, three potential marks.

Data and uncertainties — the quiet mark-leak

Paper 2 (and Paper 3) reward correct handling of uncertainty, and it's where many otherwise-strong scripts drop points:

  • Quote uncertainties to one significant figure, and match the value's decimal place to it.
  • When reading a graph, the gradient carries an uncertainty too — use the steepest/shallowest lines through the error bars.
  • Propagate correctly: add absolute uncertainties when adding quantities, add fractional/percentage uncertainties when multiplying or dividing.

Paper 2 checklist

  • Did I answer the command term, not just the topic?
  • For calculations: principle → equation → substitution → answer with units?
  • For "explain": is there an explicit because / therefore?
  • Sig-figs and units consistent throughout?
  • Uncertainties to 1 s.f., propagated the right way?
